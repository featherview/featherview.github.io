Featherview is an image viewer for the Mac that keeps up with your fingers. Open one picture, flip through the whole folder, toss the rejects, batch-convert the keepers.

Download 0.12.1 Follow on GitHub

Free and open source, for macOS Sonoma and later. First open needs one Terminal line:

Opening it the first time

Drag Featherview to Applications, then paste this into Terminal once. It works on any account, administrator or not.

Administrator? System Settings › Privacy & Security › Open Anyway works too.

Or install with Homebrew, which opens it straight away:

Why the extra step? Featherview is made in our spare time and given away for free. For macOS to open it straight away, Apple has to notarize it, and that costs 99 € / $99 every year. If Featherview turns out useful to you, a small contribution towards that fee would mean a lot. Thank you for trying it either way.

the viewer

Everything you need to sort a folder of photos. Nothing in the way.

One window, the picture edge to edge, and your keyboard. Panels appear only when you ask.

It’s just there

Double-click a photo in Finder. It opens, and the rest of the folder lines up behind it.

One key, one job

Rotate, zoom, trash, full screen. Each is a single key, no finger yoga. Mouse fans get a toolbar.

Rotation without regret

R and L turn JPEG, HEIC and TIFF without touching a single pixel.

Hard to break

Delete goes to the Trash. Batch output never overwrites. One broken file never stops the show.

Nosy, on request

Press I for the histogram, the camera and exposure, and where the shot was taken, in your language and worked out on your Mac, with nothing sent anywhere.

Mac to the core

Real Mac menus, full screen, Open With, drag and drop, true colours, and HDR that glows on HDR screens.

batch

A whole shoot in. Exactly what you need out.

Pick the keepers, choose a format, a size and a name, add the steps you want, watch the preview follow along, press Convert. Keep the recipe as a preset for next time.

  • Never overwrites a fileYour originals stay exactly as they were.
  • Keeps the storyCamera details and location come along.
  • Doesn’t give upA file it can’t read is skipped. The rest still gets done.

Resize, crop, rotate, fix colour, sharpen, add a border, stamp text or a watermark, and rename with a pattern.
